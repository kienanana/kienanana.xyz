Most French **descriptive adjectives go after the noun**. Some common adjectives usually go **before** it. This differs from English, where adjectives normally precede the noun.

| Position                  | French structure           | Example               | English             |
| ------------------------- | -------------------------- | --------------------- | ------------------- |
| After the noun            | noun + adjective           | une robe rouge        | a red dress         |
| Before the noun           | adjective + noun           | une petite maison     | a small house       |
| After a verb such as être | subject + être + adjective | La maison est petite. | The house is small. |

Placement does not remove the need for **gender and number agreement**. See [[Masculin et Féminin]].

## After the noun: the usual position

| Type | Example | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Colour | une chemise noire | a black shirt |
| Nationality | un étudiant français | a French student |
| Shape | une table ronde | a round table |
| Other descriptions | un appartement lumineux | a bright apartment |

Examples from [[School/LAF2201/TUT/TUT 9|TUT 9]] include **des amis exceptionnels** and **des artistes engagés**.

## Before the noun: BAGS

**BAGS** helps you remember common adjectives that usually precede the noun. It is a guide to these common words, not a rule for every adjective expressing beauty, age, goodness or size.

| Category | Common adjectives | Example | English |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **B**eauty | beau / belle, joli / jolie | une belle robe | a beautiful dress |
| **A**ge | jeune, vieux / vieille, nouveau / nouvelle | un vieux livre | an old book |
| **G**oodness | bon / bonne, mauvais / mauvaise | une bonne idée | a good idea |
| **S**ize | grand / grande, petit / petite, gros / grosse | une petite maison | a small house |

For example, **magnifique** normally follows the noun: *une robe magnifique*.

You can have adjectives on both sides: **une petite maison blanche** → a small white house.

### Bel, nouvel, vieil

Before a **masculine singular noun beginning with a vowel or silent h**, use these special masculine forms:

| Usual form | Before a vowel / silent h | Example | English |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| beau | bel | un bel homme | a handsome man |
| nouveau | nouvel | un nouvel appartement | a new apartment |
| vieux | vieil | un vieil ami | an old friend |

See [[Masculin et Féminin#Beau, nouveau, vieux: special masculine forms|Masculin et Féminin]] for the feminine forms and more examples.

## Position can change the meaning

Some adjectives can appear on either side of the noun with a different meaning. Learn these as pairs.

| Adjective | Before the noun | After the noun |
| --- | --- | --- |
| grand | un grand homme → a great man | un homme grand → a tall man |
| ancien | mon ancien professeur → my former teacher | un meuble ancien → an antique piece of furniture |
| propre | ma propre chambre → my own room | une chambre propre → a clean room |
| cher | un cher ami → a dear friend | un livre cher → an expensive book |

## When the adjective follows être

The BAGS placement guide concerns adjectives **next to a noun**. After **être**, the adjective follows the verb and still agrees with the subject:

- C'est **une petite maison**. → It is a small house.
- La maison **est petite**. → The house is small.
- Les maisons **sont petites**. → The houses are small.

For comparisons such as *La maison est plus petite que l'autre*, see [[La Comparaison]].

## Quick revision

1. Most descriptive adjectives go **after the noun**.
2. Common BAGS adjectives usually go **before the noun**.
3. Check whether changing position changes the meaning.
4. Make the adjective agree with the noun in **gender and number**.

For pronunciation when an adjective comes before or after a noun, see [[School/LAF2201/TUT/TUT 9|TUT 9: liaison examples]].
